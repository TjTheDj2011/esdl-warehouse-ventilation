II. DESCRIPTION OF THE PROJECT

The system is a closed-loop thermal ventilation controller built on an ESP32-WROOM-32 microcontroller. Two DS18B20 digital temperature probes measure chamber and ambient air on separate one-wire buses. A dual-channel DRV8833 H-bridge drives two 80 mm brushless fans arranged for diagonal cross-ventilation: intake low on one wall, exhaust high on the opposite wall. Three SSD1306 OLED panels display inside temperature, outside temperature and system state. An active buzzer signals state transitions and sensor faults.

III. DESIGN OBJECTIVE

To design and prototype a ventilation system that independently monitors internal and external temperature and dynamically drives a dual-fan intake/exhaust arrangement with no human intervention.

The governing principle is to move air only when the air drawn in is cooler than the air pushed out, and otherwise to stop moving air. Stated as a performance claim: the system drives the chamber toward the lowest temperature obtainable by air exchange, and never takes an action that makes the chamber hotter.

C. Analytical design calculations

Airflow per fan, from the 20 CFM rating:

V̇ = 20 × 4.7195×10−4 = 9.439×10−3 m³/s

Mass flow and the rate of sensible heat removal:

ṁ = ρV̇ = 1.204 × 9.439×10−3 = 1.136×10−2 kg/s

Q̇ = ṁ cp (Ti − To) = 11.4 · ΔT  [W]

The system therefore removes approximately 11.4 W per kelvin of inside-to-outside difference, or about 57 W at ΔT = 5 K. The same expression is negative when outside is hotter: ventilating under that condition adds heat at the same rate, which is the analytical basis for the SEALED state.

An energy balance on a well-mixed chamber with an internal source gives

Vρcp (dTi/dt) = ṁcp(To − Ti) + Qs

from which the steady state is

Ti,ss = To + Qs / (ṁcp)

With no internal source the chamber settles at ambient exactly. The system contains no refrigeration and therefore cannot cool below ambient; with a source it settles approximately 1 K above ambient per 11.4 W. Increasing airflow reduces that elevation but cannot make it negative.

The homogeneous solution is first order with

Ti(t) = To + (Ti,0 − To)e−t/τ,  τ = V / V̇

Mass and specific heat cancel, so the thermal time constant is simply the chamber volume divided by the volumetric flow rate — one air-change time.

D. Derivation of the control thresholds

The DS18B20 is specified to ±0.5 °C, i.e. ±0.90 °F. Quantisation at 12-bit resolution is 0.0625 °C, an order of magnitude smaller, so tolerance dominates. To prevent re-triggering on noise the hysteresis band must exceed the peak-to-peak uncertainty of one sensor:

band > 2 × 0.90 = 1.80 °F  →  80.0/77.0 °F gives 3.00 °F, a margin of 1.67

The differential comparison involves both probes. Worst case the tolerances add to ±1.80 °F; treated as independent the root-sum-square figure is ±1.27 °F. The worst case was adopted because both probes are the same part from the same batch and their errors may be correlated rather than cancelling. The 2.0 °F entry deadband therefore clears the worst-case uncertainty, and exit at 1.0 °F provides differential hysteresis.

The supply is sized by inrush rather than steady draw. The 5 V 2 A supply provides 3.3× headroom on that transient.
